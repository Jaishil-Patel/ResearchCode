What I learned

Photogrammetry: a room from photographs

01

Capture

Heavily overlapping phone photographs — the perimeter, then the interior, then an orbit of each piece of furniture.

02

Three heights

Floor level, Pepper's camera height at about 1.2 m, and above head height. The policy sees the room from 1.2 m, and that is exactly where an eye-level-only pass is worst.

03

Reconstruct

The photographs are fused into a single textured mesh, exported and opened in Blender 4.5.

A real room, captured and brought into Blender as a draft model — the path works end to end. No tape measurements were taken on the day, so its metric scale is still unverified.
What I learned

Cleaning the scan in Blender

A raw capture arrives with stray geometry and floating debris around the room it was meant to reconstruct. Clearing that away is what turns a scan into something a simulator can use.

The cleaned scan becomes a world

Pepper in room.mp4
Pepper standing in the scanned room

Simulation

The cleaned mesh is imported twice over: a textured mesh of 27,255 triangles for the camera, and a separate 4,999-triangle collision mesh with the ceiling stripped away for the physics engine. Pepper is then spawned into it and stands on the scanned floor.

This is the demo capture, not the room the experiment will use — a corridor of about 9 by 14 m, with no tape measurements behind it. Getting it to load is the result; trusting its dimensions is not.

Where the project is

Pepper takes commands — on the bench, not yet on the robot

01

Two interpreters

Pepper's NAOqi SDK is Python 2 only. ROS 2 Humble is Python 3.10. The two cannot share a process.

02

A shim between them

Every NAOqi call is quarantined in a Python 2 side-car, reachable over sockets: commands on UDP 45455, the depth stream on TCP 45456.

03

One motion, two robots

The real wave imports its waypoints from the simulated one — NAOqi and the URDF use identical joint names, so retiming it changes both.

None of it has touched a real robot yet. Wake, move, stop, rest and the depth stream are all verified against a fake shim; the NAOqi calls themselves meet reality for the first time on the day.
Next step

Capture the room for real

01

Capture

The actual deployment room, with at least eight tape measurements across all three axes, taken on the same visit.

02

Clean & scale

The same Blender pass as shown, then scaled to true metric dimensions and checked against the tape.

03

Validate

Check the reconstruction against the tape. Nothing has been through that gate yet, so no room has earned its dimensions.

04

Run on Pepper

Install the Python 2 side, check her NAOqi version, and move the real robot once.

Then SAC training in both worlds — the photogrammetric room and the hand-made one — which is the comparison the whole project turns on.
